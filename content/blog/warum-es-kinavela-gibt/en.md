On 15 August 2026, our home page opens with a deliberately specific line: “For Cameroonian families in Germany.” Kinavela is not trying to become another network for everyone. It starts with families who feel at home among several places, languages, and habits, yet often find people nearby with a similar story only by chance.

From Mainz, we keep seeing the same simple gap. A family can have a strong network in Germany and still know nobody nearby who shares certain memories, dishes, or languages. A call to relatives in Cameroon keeps people close over many kilometres. It does not replace the family you can meet on a free afternoon.

Kinavela should make that first step easier.

## Near, without revealing an address

That is why discovery on Kinavela begins with an approximate distance. Families choose the radius in which they want to find others. The platform needs to understand which families might be a good match. It does not need to show anyone exactly where another family lives.

This boundary was one of our first product decisions. Contact details, precise locations, and child profiles do not belong in public discovery. People see only enough to decide that getting to know one another might make sense.

Both families then have to agree. A private conversation becomes possible only when each family wants the connection. It takes one step more than sending an open message. We do not see that step as friction; it is where trust begins.

## Culture lives in ordinary days

Cultural roots are not a collection kept for special occasions. They live in small things: an expression a child understands for the first time, a recipe cooked without weighing its ingredients, or the story behind a family name.

This is why Kinavela has Villages and cultural missions. A Village is a small community built by families. A mission can be as simple as bringing a word, a memory, or a dish into family life. None of it needs to happen in front of an unknown audience.

We are not building Kinavela so families spend more time on a platform. It has done its job when an approximate distance becomes a familiar conversation, that conversation becomes a meeting, and separate families grow into a community that supports them away from the screen too.

## Kinavela in four steps

1. A family creates its profile and chooses which details are visible during discovery.
2. It selects a radius and sees compatible families at approximate distances, without exact addresses.
3. A connection request opens a private conversation only after both families agree.
4. Connected families can build a Village and bring culture into everyday life through shared activities or missions.

This order separates discovery, consent, and private conversation. A family does not reveal everything at once; it makes a new choice at each step.

Kinavela begins with Cameroonian families in Germany. The wider idea is open: people should not have to trade their roots for their new home. Both can belong to the same everyday life.
